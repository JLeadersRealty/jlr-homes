Council Bluffs in 60 Seconds

Iowa address. Omaha skyline. Room to spread out.

Council Bluffs is a city of about 62,800 people on the Iowa side of the Missouri River, directly across from downtown Omaha. It's the county seat of Pottawattamie County and sits at the southern edge of the Loess Hills, so you can live on a wooded acreage with valley views and still be at a Creighton game or the Old Market in about 15 minutes. Buyers come here for more house and more land for the money than most of the Omaha metro, strong school options, and a growing local economy anchored by one of Google's largest data center campuses.

Luxury Homes in Council Bluffs

Four ways to live well in Council Bluffs

Luxury here doesn't look like a gated subdivision. It looks like five acres of oak timber, a walk-out with a view of the valley, or a 1890s home with original woodwork. Here's where we see it.

01

Loess Hills acreages

East of town the land rolls up into the Loess Hills, one of only two places on Earth with wind-blown soil bluffs this deep. Acreage neighborhoods off the county blacktops give you one to five acres, big skies and sunsets, with town 10 minutes away.

  • Typical lot1 to 5+ acres
  • Watch forWell, septic, rural water, private road agreements
  • SchoolsVaries by address. Always verify the district before you buy.

Estate & legacy properties

03

Established in-town neighborhoods

Mature trees, larger lots and executive homes on the hillsides inside city limits. You get a quiet cul-de-sac and city utilities, and you're minutes from I-29, I-80 and the bridge to Omaha.

  • Where we've soldSunset Dr, Parkwild, Woodridge Ct, Hemlock St

04

Historic homes on the bluffs

Council Bluffs has some of the best Victorian and early-1900s architecture in the metro, tucked along the bluffs near downtown. Original woodwork, pocket doors and wraparound porches, often for a fraction of what similar homes cost across the river.

  • Known forVictorian, Queen Anne and Craftsman homes
  • Watch forOld wiring, foundations, lead paint disclosures, historic district rules
  • Good to knowAsk us about inspectors who know old houses

We represented the buyer on 515 E Pierce St in 2020, then listed and sold it again in 2022.

Jamie's Insider Take
Acreages out east can straddle three school districts. Underwood, Treynor and Lewis Central all meet out there, sometimes on the same road. Two houses side by side can go to different schools, so we check the exact address before you fall in love.
Most acreages run on a private septic system. That takes more know-how than a city sewer line: inspections, county rules, tank and field condition. Ask any agent you're talking to how many septic systems they've walked a buyer through.
Close to the river doesn't automatically mean flood insurance. Some of the flood zones in Council Bluffs are right in the middle of town, and plenty of homes nearer the river aren't in one. We pull the FEMA flood map for the address so you know before you make an offer.
It's called Council Bluffs for a reason. The hills scare some buyers off, but they're also where the views and walk-outs are. We look hard at drainage, grading, retaining walls and the foundation, and bring in the right inspector when a hillside lot needs a closer look.

Living in Council Bluffs

What buyers ask us most

Schools

Two public districts serve the city: Council Bluffs Community School District (Abraham Lincoln and Thomas Jefferson high schools) and Lewis Central on the south and east side. St. Albert is the Catholic option. Acreages east of town can fall into Underwood, Treynor or Lewis Central. Always verify the district for a specific address.

Commute

Downtown Omaha is about 10 to 15 minutes across the I-480 bridge. I-29 and I-80 meet here, so west Omaha, the airport and Lincoln are all an easy drive. Out on an acreage, add about 10 minutes.

Outdoors

Lake Manawa State Park for boating and trails, the Loess Hills Scenic Byway out your back door, and the Bob Kerrey Pedestrian Bridge to walk or bike over to Omaha's riverfront.

Jobs & growth

Google has invested more than $5 billion in its Council Bluffs data center campus, and a second big campus, Edged's Project Lola, is going up right now near Iowa Western off I-80. Add healthcare, logistics, the casinos and the whole Omaha job market across the river.

Iowa side, Omaha life

If you live in Iowa and work in Nebraska, you'll usually file in both states. It's worth a quick talk with a tax pro before you buy. We're not CPAs, but we know good ones.

Things to know

Hillside lots mean walk-outs (great) and drainage questions (ask). Parts of the river bottoms are in flood zones, so check flood maps and insurance quotes early.

Council Bluffs FAQ

Questions about living in Council Bluffs

Is Council Bluffs a good place to buy a luxury home?

Yes, especially if you want land, views or character. Luxury in Council Bluffs usually means Loess Hills acreages, custom homes with walk-out lower levels, or historic homes near downtown, and you'll typically get more house and more land than at a similar price in west Omaha. The top of the market is small, so many of the best properties sell through agent networks before they get much online attention.

How far is Council Bluffs from downtown Omaha?

About 10 to 15 minutes by car across the I-480 bridge, depending on traffic and where in Council Bluffs you start. Eppley Airfield is roughly 15 minutes away. Acreages east of town usually add about 10 minutes.

What school districts serve Council Bluffs?

Council Bluffs Community School District and Lewis Central Community School District serve the city, and St. Albert offers Catholic K-12. Acreages east of town can straddle the Underwood, Treynor and Lewis Central districts. School boundaries follow specific addresses, so always confirm the district for the home you're considering.

Is Council Bluffs a buyer's or seller's market right now?

A seller's market. Over the last 12 months, 843 Council Bluffs homes sold with a median price of $236,250 and a median of just 8 days on market, and there's about 1.8 months of inventory (under 3 months usually means sellers have the edge). The $500K+ segment is active too, with 67 sales in that range. Source: SWIAR MLS, November 2025 through November 2026.

Do I need flood insurance in Council Bluffs?

Not necessarily. Being close to the Missouri River doesn't automatically put a home in a flood zone, and some Council Bluffs flood zones are in the middle of town. Lenders require flood insurance only when a home is in a FEMA special flood hazard area, so check the FEMA flood map for the exact address before you make an offer.

Can I buy an acreage near Council Bluffs?

Yes. The Loess Hills east and north of town have acreage neighborhoods with one to five acres and larger parcels further out. Before you buy, check the well and septic, whether it's on rural water, any shared road agreements, the survey, and zoning for outbuildings or animals.

What is there to do in Council Bluffs?

Plenty. Fish, boat and hit the beach at Lake Manawa State Park, go out on the 100 block of West Broadway (don't miss the drag brunch at Caddy's), have dinner at 712 Eat + Drink, grab coffee at local shops like Louie's and Frosted Pine, try the Horseshoe, Harrah's and Ameristar casinos, drive the Loess Hills Scenic Byway, or walk the Bob Kerrey Pedestrian Bridge over to Omaha. A new $200 million Hollywood Casino Hotel, replacing the Ameristar riverboat, is expected to open in 2028.

If I live in Council Bluffs and work in Omaha, which state do I pay income tax to?

Usually both. You'd typically file a Nebraska nonresident return for wages earned there and an Iowa resident return, with a credit to avoid paying twice on the same income. Rules change, so talk with a tax professional about your situation.
