Neola in 60 Seconds

Small-town schools. Big-city access.

Neola is a town of about 900 people in Pottawattamie County, Iowa, that got its start in 1869 when the Rock Island railroad came through. It sits right off I-80, about 20 minutes east of Council Bluffs and 30 minutes from downtown Omaha, and it's home to Tri-Center High School in the Tri-Center Community School District. Buyers come for family-friendly neighborhoods like Mulberry Circle, older homes with character, and acreages in the rolling country all around town. Meyering Addition on the edge of town keeps adding new building lots, and the City of Neola runs housing incentive programs for new construction and home purchases.

Living in Neola

What buyers ask us most

Schools

Neola is home to Tri-Center High School, part of the Tri-Center Community School District. St. Albert Catholic Schools in Council Bluffs also takes students from Neola. Acreages outside town can fall into other districts, so always verify the address.

Commute

Neola is right off I-80. Council Bluffs is about 20 minutes, downtown Omaha about 30, and the I-880 interchange is just down the road for heading north.

Housing programs

The City of Neola offers forgivable loans of up to $15,000 to build new and $5,000 to buy an existing home, plus 0% rehab loans. See the programs. Call the city at 712-343-2424, or ask us and we'll walk you through it.

Outdoors

In town, there's a dog park and a walking path that loops the city sports complex, with a park in the middle. Just outside town, Arrowhead Park has a 17-acre fishing lake, camping, cabins and trails you can hike or ride horses on.

Groceries & errands

More than you'd expect in a town this size: a grocery store, Charlie Lou's coffee shop, a bank, a barber shop, Sidelines for sports apparel, an attorney, the NACC gym, Buck Snort for food, two bars, a volunteer-run movie theater and an event center that opened this year.

Things to know

Neola homes rarely sit long, especially in the family neighborhoods. Get on our private list (below) if Neola is your target.

City of Neola Housing Programs

Neola will help you build, buy or fix up

The City of Neola runs four housing programs. Here's the short version. The fine print is in the city's guidelines.

Build new: up to $15,000

A five-year forgivable loan of $12.50 per finished square foot, up to $15,000, for households at or below 120% of the county median income. Earn more than that? You can still get $6.25 per square foot, up to $7,500. It's forgiven as you live there as your primary home. Builders selling to qualifying buyers can get a 2.5% credit, up to $3,500.

Buy an existing home: $5,000

A five-year forgivable loan of $5,000 for households at or below 120% of the county median income. You can't have owned a Neola home in the last three years, unless you're moving up to a home worth 150% of your current one.

Fix it up: up to $10,000 at 0%

Owner-occupied homes at or below 100% of the county median income can get a no-interest rehab loan of up to $10,000 over as long as ten years, with a 20% cash match. Rentals can get up to $5,000 with a 50% match. It can be combined with the existing-home program.

Tear down an eyesore

The city helps property owners demolish dilapidated buildings, which can include the fire department doing a training burn on the structure.

Apply before you start

The City Council approves every application, and nothing can be approved once construction has started. Loans run through the bank in Neola (Farmers & Merchants, now Midstates Bank) or another approved lender. Incentives may count as taxable income, so check with your tax advisor.

We'll walk you through it

We know Neola and these programs, and we'll help you line up the house or lot, the lender and the application. City questions go to Neola City Hall at 712-343-2424.

Things to Do in Neola

Small town, big backyard

What we'd show you on your first Saturday in town.

Jamie's pick

Hoo Doo Days

A must-do. Neola's hometown celebration started as a welcome home for World War II soldiers and is going on 80 years strong, with a barbecue contest, family events, the Hoo Doo Hustle 5K and one of the biggest small-town parades around.

Labor Day weekend

Arrowhead Park

A 17-acre lake stocked with bluegill, bass and catfish, canoe and paddleboat rentals, 65 campsites, rustic cabins and hiking and horse trails, open year-round. Every October it hosts the Arrowhead Park Chili Fest.

Just outside town

Friday nights with the Trojans

Tri-Center football won the 2024 Class A state championship, the first state title in school history, the same year the area was recovering from the Minden tornado. Friday nights here are a big deal.

2024 state champions

Food trucks at the Blue Cow Pavilion

In the summer, food trucks roll into Neola's Blue Cow Pavilion with something different to try. Grab dinner to take home or eat under the pavilion with the neighbors.

Summer months

The dog park & walking path

The dog park sits right next to the city's sports complex, with a walking path that loops the whole thing and a park in the middle. Walk anytime, or get your steps in while the kids are at practice.

City sports complex

Easy day trips

Council Bluffs' casinos, Lake Manawa and the 100 block are about 20 minutes west, and Minden's prime rib nights are just up the road.

Neola FAQ

Questions about living in Neola, Iowa

Where is Neola, Iowa?

Neola is a town of about 900 people in Pottawattamie County in southwest Iowa, right off I-80. It's about 20 minutes east of Council Bluffs and about 30 minutes from downtown Omaha, between Council Bluffs and Minden.

What school district is Neola in?

Neola is in the Tri-Center Community School District, and Tri-Center High School is in Neola. The Trojans won the 2024 Class A state football championship. St. Albert Catholic Schools in Council Bluffs also takes students from Neola. Acreages outside town can fall into other districts, so confirm the district for a specific address.

Does Neola offer incentives for building or buying a home?

Yes. The City of Neola offers a five-year forgivable loan of $12.50 per finished square foot (up to $15,000) for building a new home, a $5,000 forgivable loan for buying an existing home, no-interest rehab loans of up to $10,000 and help with dilapidated buildings. Income limits apply, and you must apply before construction starts. Purchase and new construction programs run through the local bank in Neola, now Midstates Bank (formerly Farmers & Merchants State Bank), and some have income requirements. Call the city at 712-343-2424 for current details. If you want to build, Meyering Addition on the edge of town up 5th Street keeps adding new lots.

Are there acreages for sale near Neola?

Yes. The hills around Neola have some of the best acreage ground in Pottawattamie County, on roads like Westgate, York and State Ln. Before you buy, check the septic Time of Transfer inspection, the well, road maintenance and the school district for that exact address.

What is there to do in Neola?

Arrowhead Park, just outside town, has a 17-acre fishing lake, camping, cabins and trails, plus a chili fest every October. In town there's a dog park and walking path around the sports complex, and summer food trucks at the Blue Cow Pavilion. Labor Day weekend brings Hoo Doo Days, Neola's 80-year-old hometown celebration with a barbecue contest and a big parade. Friday nights mean Tri-Center football (2024 state champs), and Council Bluffs' restaurants, casinos and Lake Manawa are about 20 minutes away. In town you'll find a grocery store, Charlie Lou's coffee shop, the NACC gym, Buck Snort, two bars, a volunteer-run movie theater and a new event center.
